Two verbs often go together. **The first verb decides the form of the second verb.** That is a **verb pattern**. Choose verb 1 above and watch verb 2 change.

Verb 2 can only take three forms: the **-ING form** (going), **TO + verb** (to go) or the **base verb** (go).

### Why is this hard for Spanish speakers?

In Spanish, verb 2 is almost always the infinitive. In English, it changes depending on verb 1.

<Contrast

  ]}
/>

<Tip title="There is no magic rule">
  Learn each verb with its partner, like a package: not just **enjoy**, but **enjoy + -ING**. Negative? Put
  **not** before verb 2: I decided **not to go**. I regret **not studying**.
</Tip>

</Section>

<Tip title="After a preposition, always -ING">
  About, of, for, in, on, from... She worries about **failing**. He insisted on **paying**. Thanks for
  **helping** me.
</Tip>

<Tip kind="careful" title='Careful with "to"'>
  In **look forward to** and **be used to**, "to" is a preposition, so -ING follows: I look forward to
  **meeting** you. I'm used to **working** at night.
</Tip>

<Section id="pattern-3" pattern={3} title="Verb + object + TO + verb" formula="verb 1 + object + [to + verb]">

<Section id="pattern-6" pattern={6} title="-ING or TO, different meaning" formula="verb 1 + [verb-ING] or [to + verb]">

Only **five verbs** (remember, forget, try, stop, regret), but they are important: the form changes the meaning. Switch between the two sentences.

<StopTimeline />

<Tip title="Shortcut">
  **TO**: verb 2 comes **after** verb 1. **-ING**: verb 2 came **before**, or was already happening.
  **Exception: try.**
</Tip>

<Section id="mistakes" title="Common mistakes">

These are the mistakes Spanish speakers make most often:

- **"que" sentences:** I want **you to help** me. Not: I want that you help me.
- **Translating the Spanish infinitive:** I enjoy **cooking**. Not: I enjoy to cook.
- **"to" that is a preposition:** I look forward to **seeing** you. Not: I look forward to see you.
- **"to" after make or let:** My boss made me **work** late. Not: My boss made me to work late.
